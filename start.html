Mystery clue game

You read a short detective story a paragraph at a time and keep a timeline of what happened: who was where, who said what, and how far you believe each of them. Before the story gives an answer away, you show it: link the evidence and circle the answer it leaves.

How to play

Short cases

About two minutes each. Each turns on one move of the game; start anywhere.

  • The Lamp-Keeper's WordOne witness's word reaches you by two people.
  • The Rent BoxTwo lodgers' statements that cannot both be true.
  • The Rent Box PapersThe same case, its clues as papers you read in any order.
  • The Last FerryWhat happened in what order.
  • The Stable ClockOne shot, two honest witnesses, two times.
  • The Ring on the BenchA locked room, and the ways into it.
  • The Cellar DoorWhat an act needed, and who had it.
  • The Dinner at Larch HouseOne evening, told by two people.
  • The Will in the BibleA paper that says more than it could have known.
  • The Grey CloakAn honest witness who is wrong.
  • The Night Porter's StatementA storyteller you cannot wholly believe.

Full cases

Two Sherlock Holmes stories by Arthur Conan Doyle (1892), each a whole story of a few hours. They are experiments, kept as they stood when the short cases began, with their own copy of the page.

  • Silver BlazeA racehorse gone, and its trainer dead on the moor.
  • The Adventure of the Speckled BandA sister's death in a locked room, two years ago.
