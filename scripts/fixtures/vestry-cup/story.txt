THE VESTRY CUP

I am Kit Sorrel, clerk of St. Ebba's. On Sunday at six o'clock, after evensong, I locked the silver cup in the vestry cupboard, locked the vestry door behind me, and took its key back to the sexton's lodge. At nine o'clock I came back to write the inventory, and the cupboard was locked and the cup was gone.

From six until nine the bells rang for practice. I sat in the church porch the whole time with the ringers' list, and I saw Amos Reed, the sexton, go up the tower stair at six and come down it at nine. The tower stair comes down into the porch, and it is the only way up or down.

The vestry door has that one key, and it hangs in the sexton's lodge; whoever borrows it signs the key book there. When I found the cup gone I took the key book away with me.

Mr. Vane, the curate, was not at evensong: he had been sent for to the Grange that afternoon. Hester Dent, the churchwarden's daughter, does the altar flowers.

On Monday morning Hester Dent brought the cup back to me. She had borrowed the vestry key at half past seven and taken the cup home to mend its dented foot, meaning it for a surprise at Easter. Amos had rung all evening, and Mr. Vane had not left the Grange.
