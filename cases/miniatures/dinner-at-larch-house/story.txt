THE DINNER AT LARCH HOUSE

George Marlow's account. On Thursday we gave a small dinner at Larch House: my wife Clara and I, Captain Voss and Miss Pym. As the gong went I put fifty pounds in notes in the drawer of the study desk. When our guests had gone the drawer was empty.

I did not leave the table from the soup to the coffee, and Hannah, our maid, was at the sideboard the whole time. Clara was called upstairs with the soup and came back as the joint was carried in. Captain Voss went out during the fish, to fetch his cigar-case from his overcoat, he said, and was gone some minutes. Miss Pym went out during the dessert, to speak to her coachman. One of our two guests took my notes, and I have asked my wife to set down what she saw.

Clara Marlow's account. From the soup until the joint I sat with the baby on the nursery landing, which looks down on the hall and on the study door.

During the fish Captain Voss came out of the dining-room. He took something from the pocket of his overcoat on the hall stand and went straight back in. He was not out of my sight for a moment, and he did not go near the study door. Nobody else crossed the hall while I sat there.

I went down as the joint was carried in. After that there was nobody to watch the hall.

George Marlow again. The notes came back by Friday's first post, with a letter from Miss Pym that I shall not copy here.
