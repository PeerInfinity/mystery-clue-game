THE GARDEN ROOM

I am Ellen Wray, and I keep house for my uncle, Mr. Tobias Wray, at Holm Lodge. On Saturday at four o'clock he laid his gold repeater on the table in the garden room, as he does every afternoon, and went up to rest. When the gong went at eight the watch was gone.

The garden room has two doors and no window that opens. One door gives on the passage and the other on the terrace. Only two others were about the house that evening, my cousin Philip Wray and our neighbour Mr. Farrant, and one of those two took the watch.

From four until the gong I sat sewing in the window-seat of the passage, with the passage door of the garden room in front of me. It did not open once in those four hours. Philip sat by me, winding my wool, until the lamps were lit at dusk. Then he said he would take a turn in the garden, and I did not see him again until the gong. As he went, Mr. Farrant came in by the front door, and he stayed talking with me in the passage until eight.

Dunn, the gardener, told me the rest on Sunday. "I was clipping the yew on the terrace from four until dusk, miss, with the terrace door of the garden room straight before me. It was shut the whole time. Mr. Farrant came up the drive at five and walked the terrace with his cigar until I put up my shears. A dozen times he passed that door, and he never laid a hand on it. At dusk I went home to my tea."

On Monday the watch was found at a pawnbroker's in Castle Street, pledged on Saturday night in Philip's name, and he owned to it.
