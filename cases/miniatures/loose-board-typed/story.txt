THE LOOSE BOARD, TOLD FROM ITS DATA

I am Ann Tolley, the keeper of the draper's shop in Fore Street. The cash-box was in the hiding-place under the loose board. Jane Marsh is my sister. Last month, I sent the letter about the hiding-place to Jane Marsh. From last month, Jane Marsh knew the hiding-place under the loose board. The hiding-place under the loose board was a secret told to one person only.

From two o'clock on Wednesday until three o'clock on Wednesday, I was at St. Mary's. Jane Marsh was at St. Mary's. At three o'clock on Wednesday, the cash-box was not in the hiding-place under the loose board. Between two o'clock on Wednesday and three o'clock on Wednesday, somebody took the cash-box from the draper's shop in Fore Street. Who took the cash-box?

At three o'clock on Wednesday, the draper's shop in Fore Street was untouched but for the loose board, the till with two pounds of silver as it was left.

Dick is my errand boy. From one o'clock on Wednesday until four o'clock on Wednesday, Dick was at Hilltop.

Jane Marsh told me: "When the letter came, I read aloud the letter about the hiding-place at my house. Mr. Voss is my lodger. When the letter came, Mr. Voss was at my house."

Afterwards, the cash-box was in Mr. Voss's trunk. Mr. Voss said to me: "It was I who took the cash-box from the draper's shop in Fore Street."
