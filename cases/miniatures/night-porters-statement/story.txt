THE NIGHT PORTER'S STATEMENT

Inspector Reeve's note. On Friday morning the strongroom of Bly's Bank was found open, and four hundred pounds gone. I had Josiah Kemp, the night porter, write down his night for me. His statement follows, and after it what I know myself.

Josiah Kemp's statement. I came on at ten on Thursday night. Mr. Bly gave me the keys of the bank and of the strongroom, and from that minute until the morning they never left my belt.

At one o'clock I heard a step outside and went to look. Tobias Finch, the clerk who was turned off last month, was standing at the window of the bank, looking in. He ran when he saw my lamp. I think it right to say so.

Inspector Reeve again. I examined the street door and the strongroom door myself. Neither had been forced: both were opened with their keys.

As for Tobias Finch, he has been in my own lock-up at Cross Street since Wednesday, for a debt. I turned the key on him myself at ten on Thursday night, and let him out to his breakfast at seven on Friday.

I showed Kemp the lock-up book. The four hundred pounds were under the coals in the porter's box.
