THE WATCHMAN'S CLOCK

I am Edwin Marsh, chief clerk at Lowther's warehouse on Quay Street. At eight on Monday night I locked the yard with a bale of China silk on the floor by the gate, and when I unlocked it at six on Tuesday morning the bale was gone. When was it taken, and who took it?

Our night watchman, Abel Ward, carries a tell-tale clock. By the rule of the house he must punch it at the station in the yard every hour, from twelve until five, and the clock marks each punch on a card that only I can unlock. I took Tuesday's card out of it myself.

Ward's statement to me: "I made every round, from twelve until five, and saw and heard nothing. If the bale went, it went after five, when I had gone to the lodge. Only Joe Tanner, the carter, has a key to the gate besides me."

Constable Bright, whose beat is Quay Street, told me that he tried our yard gate at half past two and found it fast, and at half past three found it on the latch.

I went to Tanner's house. He lay in bed with his ankle in splints, set by the surgeon on Saturday, and could not have stood under a bale of silk.

When I laid the card in front of Ward, he owned that at three he had carried the bale out to a cart in the lane, and had locked the gate again before his round at four.
