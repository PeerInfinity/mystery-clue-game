THE SAILOR'S KNOT, TOLD FROM ITS DATA

I am John Hale, a guest at Fernleigh, twenty years at sea. At nine o'clock on Thursday, the silver plate was in the dining-room. At half past ten on Thursday, the silver plate was in the bundle. The bundle was in the shrubbery. Between nine o'clock on Thursday and half past ten on Thursday, somebody tied the bundle at Fernleigh. Who tied the bundle?

At half past ten on Thursday, the bundle was tied with the carrick bend. The carrick bend was held to be a seaman's knot, which a landsman does not tie.

Tom Pike is the cook's brother, an able seaman home on leave. Tom Pike knew the carrick bend. From Thursday afternoon until seven o'clock on Thursday, Tom Pike was in the kitchen. Sam Rudd is the groom at Fernleigh. Ellen is the parlourmaid at Fernleigh. From seven o'clock on Thursday until eleven o'clock on Thursday, Sam Rudd was at Fernleigh. Ellen was at Fernleigh.

The landlord of the Bell told me: "From seven o'clock on Thursday until eleven o'clock on Thursday, Tom Pike was at the Bell."

Sam Rudd was in the Grimsby fishing fleet for four years as a boy. Sam Rudd knew the carrick bend.

Ellen did not know the carrick bend.

Afterwards, Sam Rudd said to me: "It was I who tied the bundle at Fernleigh."
