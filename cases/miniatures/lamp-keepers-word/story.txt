THE LAMP-KEEPER'S WORD

I am Hale, constable at Saltmarsh. On Tuesday night, at about ten o'clock, the bonded store on the quay was broken open, and by Wednesday morning the town had a name ready for me: Tobias Wren, the boatman. Only one person could have seen the quay at that hour. Marta Quill keeps the harbour lamp, and from the lamp room she looks straight down on it.

Marta has had no voice since the fever. What she has to say she writes, and I have known her hand these twenty years.

On Wednesday morning Pike, the harbourmaster, brought me his report. "I went up to the lamp at first light and asked Marta what she saw. She wrote it on her slate for me. At ten o'clock Wren was on the quay, under her lamp."

At noon Elsie Quill, who is Marta's niece, came to my door with a basket on her arm. "My aunt cannot leave the lamp," she said, "so she sent me down with her word. At ten o'clock she saw Wren at the chapel door, up on the hill."

Wren was not in two places at once. Marta saw one thing, and either Pike or Elsie had brought me something she never wrote, unless Marta herself had written two tales.

Each of the two had a reason. Pike and Wren came to blows in the spring over the mooring dues. Elsie and Wren are to be married at Michaelmas.

On Thursday morning I took up the basket, which Elsie had left by my door, to carry it back to her. Under the cloth lay a folded paper with her name on the outside. I knew the hand. It read: "Take this to the constable. At ten Wren was on the quay, under my lamp. M. Q."

I laid the paper in front of Elsie, and she did not look at it twice. "He was on the quay," she said. "I changed it. I thought the chapel would keep him out of it."
