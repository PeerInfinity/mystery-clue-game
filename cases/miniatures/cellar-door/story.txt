THE CELLAR DOOR

I am Martha Coyle, and I keep the Anchor at Saltby. On Friday at ten o'clock I locked the cellar door myself, as I do every night. At six on Saturday morning the cellar door stood open, and the best brandy cask was a gallon lighter.

Two men about the place might have done it. Ned Sharp, the ostler, was dead drunk in the yard that morning. Jem Tully, the potman, was as sober as a judge.

The cellar door opens off the passage behind the taproom. There are two keys to it. One is on my own ring, and the other Jem carries, night and day, for the morning's barrels.

Ned sleeps in the loft over the stable, across the yard, and on Friday the carrier's man shared it with him. "He was up there before ten and never stirred till six," the carrier's man told me. "I had to climb over him to get out."

Jem sleeps on the settle in the passage, from ten until I come down at six.

I asked Jem for his key. He gave it up, and the money with it: he had sold a gallon to the carrier's man at midnight, and stood Ned a mugful at dawn so that I should look no further than the yard.
