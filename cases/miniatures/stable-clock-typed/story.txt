THE STABLE CLOCK, TOLD FROM ITS DATA

I am Arthur Penrose, a guest at Coldharbour Hall. Mr. Edwin Royle is the nephew of Sir Giles Royle. From Thursday evening until ten minutes to nine on Thursday, I was at the library. Mr. Edwin Royle was at the library. At ten minutes to nine on Thursday by the hall clock, Mr. Edwin Royle left the library.

Somebody fired a shot through the window of Sir Giles's study. On Friday morning, the bullet was at Sir Giles's study. When was the shot fired?

Susan is the housemaid. Susan told me: "At nine o'clock on Thursday, I was at the back stairs. At nine o'clock on Thursday by the hall clock, somebody fired a shot through the window of Sir Giles's study. At nine o'clock on Thursday, I counted the strokes of the hall clock."

Bowker is the gardener. Bowker told me: "From Thursday evening, I was at the lodge. At a quarter to nine on Thursday by the stable clock, somebody fired a shot through the window of Sir Giles's study. I looked at the stable clock." Was Mr. Edwin Royle at the library when the shot was fired?

From Thursday morning, the hall clock was right, set by the church clock.

On Friday morning, the stable clock was a quarter of an hour slow.

On Friday afternoon, Mr. Edwin Royle said to the inspector: "It was nine o'clock." Mr. Edwin Royle said to the inspector: "At nine o'clock on Thursday, I was not at the library."
