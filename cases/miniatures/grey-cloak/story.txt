THE GREY CLOAK

I am Sergeant Tull, of Wickham. On Wednesday morning I found the cash drawer of Dr. Mott's surgery forced, and the week's takings gone.

Mrs. Lunn lives opposite the side door of the surgery. "At nine o'clock last night I saw a figure come out at the side door and go off down Mill Lane," she told me. "It was Dr. Mott. It was dark, but I knew him by his long grey cloak." So who was it that she saw?

But at nine o'clock on Tuesday Dr. Mott was at Squire Hale's bedside, three miles off. I can swear to it, for I sat there with him from eight until ten.

He had driven out in his greatcoat, for the rain. On Wednesday I found the grey cloak on its peg in the surgery passage, and it was still damp.

Two people beside the doctor have a key to the side door: Mr. Pell, the dispenser, and Kitty, the housemaid, who is hardly five feet tall. I went back to Mrs. Lunn. "The cloak hung on that figure as it hangs on the doctor," she said, "well clear of the ground."

Mr. Pell did not deny it long. He had put on the cloak, he said, because the whole lane knew it for the doctor's.
