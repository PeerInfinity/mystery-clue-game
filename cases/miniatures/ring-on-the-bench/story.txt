THE RING ON THE BENCH

I am Esther Vane, goldsmith, of Fetter Court. On Thursday night I left a customer's ring on the bench in my workroom, and in the morning the ring was gone. The door of the workroom was locked all night, and the key hung at my belt.

My apprentice, Tom Pike, knew the ring was there. But the window of the workroom is barred, the bars a hand's breadth apart.

The walls of the workroom are brick, and sound.

Yet the gold filings I had left in a heap on the bench were scattered. A draught from the yard had blown through the workroom that night.

Signor Belli, the organ-grinder, lodges across the yard. He keeps a monkey, and lets it run in the yard after dark.

Behind the blind I found how the draught had come. A ventilating pane above the window stood open to the yard, a gap no wider than my hand.

The cord of the blind hung from a roller beside the pane.

The cord reached down to the bench, which is screwed to the floor.

It was the monkey. Signor Belli found the ring in its bedding, with two of his own shirt-studs, and brought it across the yard with many apologies.
