THE WAGES BAG, TOLD FROM ITS DATA

I am Samuel Orchard, the clerk at Garth's Mill. At eight o'clock on Friday evening, the wages bag was in the counting-room. The counting-room door was locked. The counting-room key was in the passage. From a little after eight on Friday evening until ten to nine on Friday evening, I was at the village. At nine o'clock on Friday evening, the counting-room door was locked. The counting-room key was in the passage. The wages bag was not in the counting-room. Between eight o'clock on Friday evening and nine o'clock on Friday evening, somebody took the wages bag from the counting-room. Who took the wages bag?

Mr. Wicks is the foreman, living at Garth's Mill. Job Tarrant is the carter, living at Garth's Mill. Job Tarrant told me: "From eight o'clock on Friday evening until nine o'clock on Friday evening, I was at the forge." At ten to nine on Friday evening, Job Tarrant was not at the forge.

On Saturday morning, the counting-room lantern was in the cart-shed. Job Tarrant told me: "A little after eight on Friday evening, I had the counting-room key. I was in the counting-room. At half past eight on Friday evening, Mr. Wicks was in the counting-room." Where was Mr. Wicks at half past eight on Friday evening?

Mrs. Dabney is the cook at the mill house. Mrs. Dabney told me: "From eight o'clock on Friday evening until nine o'clock on Friday evening, Mr. Wicks was in my kitchen."

Mr. Lisle is the curate. Mr. Lisle told me: "At half past eight on Friday evening, I was in the mill lane. Mr. Wicks was in Mrs. Dabney's kitchen."

On Saturday afternoon, the wages bag was in Job Tarrant's cart. Job Tarrant said to the constable: "It was I who took the wages bag from the counting-room." Job Tarrant said to the constable: "At half past eight on Friday evening, Mr. Wicks was in Mrs. Dabney's kitchen."
