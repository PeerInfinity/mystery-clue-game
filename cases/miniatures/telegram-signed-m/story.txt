THE TELEGRAM SIGNED M.

I am Dr. Ellis, of Thornby. At eleven on Saturday night I was fetched to Dyer's Mill, where Mr. Joseph Holt, its owner, lay struck down by the loom-room door. He lived, but for a week he could tell us nothing.

In his pocket was a telegram, which I copied out. He had gone to the mill because of it, and whoever sent it had brought him there. Who sent it, and who was waiting for him at the mill?

His wife is Mary, and his partner in the mill is Mr. Martin Grove.

Mrs. Holt opened the church fête at three that afternoon, and gave the prizes at five. I was at her side in the tea tent between, the whole two hours.

Mr. Grove's clerk told me that his master took the two o'clock train to Leeds, and did not say why.

The mill's night book shows one key drawn that evening, at half past nine: Mr. Grove's.

Mr. Grove was taken at Leeds on the Monday, and owned that he had sent the telegram and waited for his partner in the dark.
