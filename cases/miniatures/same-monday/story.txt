THE SAME MONDAY

I am Ned Arliss, clerk to the harbour-master at Saltby. On Sunday at six, as the horn of the Marlow coach sounded on the quay, I saw Captain Dunmore lay the Admiralty's chart of the new channel in the drawer of the harbour office and lock the office door behind us both. On Monday at nine he opened the office and found the drawer empty. Who took the chart?

It is nine o'clock on Monday, and the packet that is to carry the chart sails at noon: three hours. I have lived these three hours before. Each time the packet's bell rings I wake again at nine on this same Monday, with the drawer empty and nobody the wiser but myself. What I learn I keep; the hours I must spend again.

This time I went to the Captain before the bell and told him what I knew. Silas Finch had the chart in his sea-chest at the chandler's: he had copied the soundings for a Marlow shipowner, and meant to put it back before the office opened.
