THE LOOSE BOARD

I am Ann Tolley, and I keep the draper's shop in Fore Street. My cash-box lives under a loose board behind the counter. Last month I wrote to my sister Jane and told her where it was, in case anything should happen to me. I have never told anyone else.

On Wednesday I shut the shop from two o'clock until three, and went with Jane to our aunt's funeral at St. Mary's. She was beside me the whole hour. When I came back the board was up, and the cash-box was gone. Who took it?

Nothing else in the shop had been touched. The till, with two pounds of silver in it, was as I had left it.

Dick, my errand boy, is in and out of the shop all week. But my delivery book has him on the round to Hilltop, two miles off, from one o'clock until four, with names signed at three houses there.

Jane told me that when my letter came she read it aloud at her breakfast table, not thinking what was in it. Her lodger, Mr. Voss, was at the table.

The constable found my cash-box in Mr. Voss's trunk, with its lock broken open.
