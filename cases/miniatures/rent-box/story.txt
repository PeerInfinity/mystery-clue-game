THE RENT BOX

I am Ada Orme. I keep a lodging-house at 9 Tanner's Row, with two lodgers, Mr. Fenn and Captain Rudd, and no servant. On Tuesday at one o'clock I counted the week's rents into the box on the dining-room sideboard, four pounds and some silver. At three o'clock I went to the box for the milk money, and it was empty.

I went straight up to the Captain, who had not been out of the house all day. "I have been sitting in the back parlour, which looks on the yard," he told me. "At half past two I saw Fenn cross the yard and come in at the back door."

Mr. Fenn knocked at four o'clock, and I asked him where he had been at half past two. "At the reading room in Cable Street," he said. "I was there all the afternoon."

Mr. Fenn could not have been in Cable Street and in my yard at the same moment. One of my lodgers was telling me a thing that was not so.

So I went over my own afternoon. Mr. Fenn stood by with his hat on while I counted the rents. I let him out at the front door and locked it behind him, for the latch is broken, and the key did not leave my apron pocket until he knocked at four. Then I went to the kitchen and was baking there until three o'clock, with the back door at my elbow. It is the only way in from the yard, and it did not open once.

I sent for the constable and told him what I have set down here. He found my four pounds in the Captain's sea-chest, and the Captain, when it was put to him, owned that he had seen nobody in the yard at all.
