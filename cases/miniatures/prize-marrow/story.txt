THE PRIZE MARROW

I am Hester Vane, secretary of the flower show at Combe Regis. On the Friday night before the show somebody went into Abel Crouch's shed at the allotments and slashed his great marrow from end to end. It had taken the cup three years running, and on Saturday it could not be put on the bench at all. Who did it?

The shed has no lock, and three people were at the allotments after dark that Friday: Abel himself, his wife Martha, who came to fetch him to his supper, and George Pask, who has the next plot and has been second to that marrow three years running. Each of the three was alone there for a time. On Saturday, with Abel's marrow gone, Pask's took the cup.

I had spoken with each of them that week. On Monday George Pask said to me over his fence: "I want to beat that marrow fair, on the bench, with the judges looking on. I would not give a button for the cup any other way."

On Wednesday Martha Crouch said to me: "I want him to take the cup once more, this year, and then give up the allotment content. He will never leave it while he thinks Pask can beat him."

And on Friday morning Abel, when he had looked over the fence at Pask's marrow, said to me: "His is the bigger this year, Miss Vane. I want mine kept off that bench. I'll not stand beside it and be second."

On Sunday Abel came to my door and owned that he had done it himself, with his own pruning-knife.
