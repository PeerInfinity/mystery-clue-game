THE WATCHMAN'S CLOCK, TOLD FROM ITS DATA

I am Edwin Marsh, chief clerk at Lowther's warehouse. At eight o'clock on Monday night, the bale of China silk was in the yard. At six o'clock on Tuesday morning, the bale of China silk was not in the yard. Between eight o'clock on Monday night and six o'clock on Tuesday morning, somebody took the bale of China silk from the yard. Who took the bale of China silk?

Abel Ward is the night watchman at Lowther's warehouse. By rule, Abel Ward was to be on the round at twelve o'clock on Monday night, one o'clock on Tuesday morning, two o'clock on Tuesday morning, three o'clock on Tuesday morning, four o'clock on Tuesday morning and five o'clock on Tuesday morning. From eight o'clock on Monday night until six o'clock on Tuesday morning, Tuesday's card was in the tell-tale clock. At six o'clock on Tuesday morning, I took Tuesday's card from the tell-tale clock.

Abel Ward told me: "From twelve o'clock on Monday night until six o'clock on Tuesday morning, I was on the round at every time the rule names. I was aware of nothing amiss. Joe Tanner is the carter. Nobody but me, Joe Tanner and you had a key of the yard gate."

Constable Bright told me: "At half past two on Tuesday morning, the yard gate was locked. At half past three on Tuesday morning, the yard gate was shut." Was the bale of China silk taken between half past two and half past three, or after five?

From Saturday, Joe Tanner was in bed with an ankle in splints, and in no state to be the one who took the bale of China silk from the yard.

Later on Tuesday, Abel Ward said to me: "It was I who took the bale of China silk from the yard." Abel Ward said to me: "It was between half past two and half past three."
