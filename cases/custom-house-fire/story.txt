THE FIRE AT THE CUSTOM HOUSE

I am Henry Ashby, a Surveyor of the Board of Customs. In the night of Tuesday the fourteenth of October the Custom House at Saltmere burned. The Board's telegram reached me on Wednesday morning, and I was on the quay at Saltmere by noon.

The Custom House stands on the quay. Its bond store is a stone vault below, and the Long Room, where the books are kept and the duties paid, is above it. The watch-box of the night watch stands beside its street door, and the Anchor inn faces it across the quay.

From the quay the mole runs out to the pier-head lamp. I walked it: the lamp is a quarter of an hour from the watch-box.

The berths lie inside the quay gate. Mill Lane is in the town, outside it.

Mr. Josiah Pell is the Collector, and lives at Cliff House, a mile and a half from the quay. I know him: he sat with me before the Board in London from the first of September until the fifth.

Mr. Jonas Prout is the Collector's clerk. Silas Crouch is the night watch of the quay. Mr. Lucas Garrick, a wine merchant of the town, has brandy lying in the bond store. Captain Isaac Venn is master of the brig Marigold, which trades to Saltmere; she was gone from the harbour when I came.

The Long Room is burnt out. Its stove was cold, raked out and shut. Was the fire an accident of the stove, or was it set?

An empty lamp-oil can, painted C.H., lay by the ledger press. In the lamp room below, the oil cupboard stood open with its key in the lock, and the can's place on the shelf was empty. Did the oil come from the Custom House's own lamp room, or was it brought in from outside?

The ledger press stood open, its books pulled out into one heap before it, and the floor was burned deepest there.

The iron chest was locked and unbroken. Mr. Pell opened it before me, and the week's duty money was in it.

The bond store below was unburnt, its door locked and its seal whole. What was the fire meant to destroy: the books of the ledger press, the duty money, or the brandy in the bond store?

The lower half of the street door still stands. The bolt of its lock is drawn back, and neither the door nor its frame is marked.

The windows on the quay are barred, and the bars are whole. How did the fire-setter come into the Custom House: by the street door with a key, by forcing the door, by a window, or because the door had been left unlocked?

By the Board's rule there are two keys to the street door, the Collector's and the clerk's. On Wednesday Mr. Pell showed me his on his watch-chain, and Mr. Prout took his from his pocket.

The bond ledger was not in the press. Mr. Pell put it into my hands at Cliff House on Wednesday afternoon, unburnt. Did the fire-setter know that the bond ledger was out of the house?

The fire bell on the quay was rung in the night. On Thursday at noon I went round the clocks: the harbour office clock and the Anchor's taproom clock agreed with my watch, which I had set by railway time, and the church clock stood half an hour behind it. When was the fire bell first rung?

On Thursday I found a caped boat-cloak pushed under an upturned boat on the hard. It reeked of lamp oil, its hem was singed, and the name J. Pell was sewn inside the collar. Where was the Collector's cloak on Tuesday night: on its peg in the Custom House, or at Cliff House with him?

Where was Silas Crouch between one o'clock and the bell: on the mole at the pier-head lamp, at the Anchor, or in the Custom House?

Could Mr. Pell have been on the quay between half past twelve and two? Could Captain Venn have been ashore at Saltmere when the Custom House was fired? Could Mr. Garrick have been at Saltmere then?

Why was the Custom House burned: to destroy the bond ledger before I read it, to hide a theft of the duty money, or out of spite against the Collector?

And who set fire to the Custom House? I had some papers with me from the first. The rest came to me as my inquiries were answered, and I read each as it came.

On Saturday I put it all to Mr. Prout, and he owned it. He had sent the watch to the pier head, let himself in with his own key when he left the Anchor, heaped the books of the press and fired them with the house's oil to burn a ledger that was not there, and walked out in the Collector's cloak.
